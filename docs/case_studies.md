## Case Studies

## Assumption: Independent observations

Why this matters

If observations are not independent, standard errors and p-values can be misleading.

### Case study

Researchers analyzing fMRI data once reported statistically significant "brain activity" in a dead Atlantic salmon because thousands of voxel-wise tests were conducted without adequate correction for multiple comparisons. The study became a widely cited demonstration of why statistical assumptions and multiple-testing corrections matter.

Bennett et al. (2009).

## Limitation: Confounding

Why this matters

A group difference does not necessarily imply a biological or causal difference.

### Case study

Early studies reported gender differences in spatial cognition. Later work showed that training participants with action video games substantially reduced the observed difference, suggesting that experience, rather than innate ability alone, contributed to performance.

Feng, Spence, & Pratt (2007).

## Tradeoff: Analytic choices

Why this matters

Different reasonable analytical decisions can lead to different conclusions.

### Case study

In the NARPS project, 70 independent research teams analyzed the same neuroimaging dataset and reached different conclusions for several hypotheses despite using the same underlying data. The study highlighted the importance of documenting analytical choices and assessing the robustness of conclusions.

Botvinik-Nezer et al. (2020).
